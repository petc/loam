In May 2047, an urban-policy researcher publishes a piece arguing that The Accord's allocation model has a compounding problem: zones that decline cannot recover, because the model has no mechanism to correct downward. What goes down keeps going down.

The piece is careful. It names no sector and gives no timeframe close enough to trace. It reads, to those who notice such things, like something written to keep people out of trouble.

For most readers it is an abstraction — a comments-section argument about methodology, about causation versus correlation. A few, further down, say something closer to *I've seen this happen where I live.*

It is the first time this flaw has been stated in public rather than filed internally and marked *noted*. It changes nothing immediately. It is small. It is also, arguably, the only thing that has moved at all.

Daniel's wife. She comes to the garden with him one afternoon in May — not for the appeal forms, just to walk the row and see what has opened.

She looks at the flowers the way some people look at something they had forgotten they were allowed to want. She crouches at the bed without being invited to, the way people do here once they have decided it is safe to, and asks the question no one else has asked out loud: is this the whole point, or is there more to it than flowers?

She is the kind of person who has needed reasons bigger than the immediate thing for a long time, out of necessity. Being given permission not to seems to cost her something.

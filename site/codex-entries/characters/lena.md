Systems integration architect, Sector 2 — a high-relevance district where almost nothing becomes an anomaly, because almost nothing there is allowed to.

She first surfaces as a name only: a routine cross-division clearance audit, an access level consistent with her role, a review from eighteen months ago, no flags. The file is closed without incident — one ordinary line among two hundred, snagging on nothing anyone could name at the time.

Months later the name acquires its weight. She is Elias's daughter, forty-four years old. She believes he died in 2041 — a notice, a closure in his file, no body she ever saw. He has let her believe it for six years, telling himself the silence protects her, or protects him, or that he has simply been afraid and built a better story on top of the fear.

She does not know he is alive. She does not know he keeps a garden, or that a stranger has now heard her name spoken aloud across a bench in the evening. To The Accord, and to her own life, she remains what the archive made her: ordinary, complete, nothing about it saying otherwise.
